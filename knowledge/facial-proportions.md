# Facial Proportions

## The Rule of Thirds

The face is divided into three equal vertical sections:
1. **Upper third:** Hairline to eyebrows
2. **Middle third:** Eyebrows to bottom of nose
3. **Lower third:** Bottom of nose to chin

**Ideal:** All three sections are roughly equal in height.

### How to Measure:
- Take a front-facing photo (natural lighting, neutral expression)
- Mark hairline, brows, nose base, chin
- Measure distances (use photo editing app or ruler)
- Compare ratios

### What Deviations Mean:

**Large upper third (high forehead):**
- **Perception:** Youthful, childlike (neoteny)
- **Common in:** Women, attractive faces
- **Enhancement:** Don't hide with bangs if it's balanced and rounded (see hairstyles-guide.md)

**Small upper third (low forehead):**
- **Perception:** Masculine, mature
- **Enhancement:** Hairstyles with volume on top (add perceived height)

**Large lower third:**
- **Perception:** Masculine, dominant (in men)
- **Enhancement:** This is a strength for men — show it off with stubble or short hair

**Small lower third:**
- **Perception:** Feminine, youthful
- **Enhancement:** Beards add visual mass (for men with weak lower thirds)

## The Rule of Fifths

The face is divided horizontally into five equal sections (each the width of one eye):
1. Outer edge → outer corner of eye
2. Outer corner → inner corner of eye (one eye width)
3. Between eyes (one eye width — interocular distance)
4. Inner corner → outer corner of other eye (one eye width)
5. Outer corner of eye → outer edge

**Ideal:** Each section is equal (~one eye width).

### How to Measure:
- Measure your eye width (outer to inner corner)
- Check interocular distance (between inner corners of eyes)
- Ideal: Interocular distance = one eye width

### Deviations:

**Wide-set eyes** (interocular distance > eye width):
- **Enhancement:** Darker eyeshadow/liner on inner corners (makeup), hairstyles with volume at sides

**Close-set eyes** (interocular distance < eye width):
- **Enhancement:** Lighter inner corners, darker outer corners (makeup), avoid hairstyles that narrow the face further

## Golden Ratio (Phi = 1.618)

The golden ratio is a mathematical proportion found in nature and art, believed to be aesthetically pleasing.

### Applied to Faces:

1. **Face length to width ratio:** ~1.618:1
   - Measure from hairline to chin (length)
   - Measure widest part of face (usually cheekbones)
   - Ideal ratio: length = width × 1.618

2. **Facial thirds to each other:**
   - Middle third to lower third ≈ 1.618:1 (in some models)

3. **Eye-to-lip distance vs. nose-to-chin:**
   - From center of eyes to lips ÷ nose base to chin ≈ 1.618

4. **Mouth width to nose width:** ~1.618:1

5. **Nose length to chin length:** ~1.618:1

**Important:** The golden ratio is an *ideal*, not a requirement. Many attractive faces deviate from it. Use as a reference, not a rulebook.

## Neoclassical Canons (Additional Proportions)

### Vertical Proportions:
- **Hairline to brows = brows to nose base = nose base to chin** (Rule of Thirds)
- **Nose length:** Should be ~1/3 of face length
- **Chin height:** Should be ~1/5 to 1/4 of lower third

### Horizontal Proportions:
- **Face width:** ~5 eye widths (Rule of Fifths)
- **Mouth width:** Should align with inner edge of iris (when looking straight ahead)
- **Nose width:** Should align with inner corners of eyes

### Eye Proportions:
- **Eye width to height ratio:** ~3:1 (almond shape)
- **Palpebral fissure slant:** Slight upward tilt (outer corner slightly higher than inner)

## Facial Harmony

**Proportions matter less than harmony.** A face can deviate from "ideal" proportions but still be attractive if features are **balanced and harmonious**.

### What is Harmony?
- Features complement each other
- No single feature dominates or looks out of place
- Smooth transitions between features
- Balanced left-right symmetry

**Example:** A large nose can be attractive if the other features (eyes, lips, chin) are proportionally balanced.

## How Proportions Affect Perception

### Youthful vs. Mature:

**Youthful proportions:**
- Larger eyes (relative to face)
- Smaller nose
- Fuller lips
- Larger upper third, smaller lower third
- Rounder face

**Mature proportions:**
- Smaller eyes (relative to face)
- Larger nose
- Thinner lips
- Larger lower third
- More angular face

### Masculine vs. Feminine:

**Masculine:**
- Larger lower third (jaw + chin prominence)
- Wider face (higher facial width-to-height ratio)
- Prominent brow ridge
- Larger nose
- Thinner lips

**Feminine:**
- Balanced thirds
- Narrower face
- Smooth forehead
- Smaller nose
- Fuller lips
- Larger eyes (relative to face)

## What to Do If Your Proportions Are "Off"

### Styling Tricks:

**Large forehead:**
- Bangs (if forehead is sloped; avoid if upright and rounded — see hairstyles-guide.md)
- Contour with makeup (darker shade along hairline)

**Small lower third:**
- Beard (men) — adds visual mass
- Lip liner slightly outside natural lip line (women)
- Chin filler or implant (surgical option)

**Wide face:**
- Face-framing hair layers
- Contouring (darken outer edges)
- Glasses with vertical emphasis

**Long face:**
- Hairstyles with width (side volume)
- Horizontal elements in clothing (wide collars)
- Deep-lens glasses frames

**Close-set eyes:**
- Lighter inner corner makeup
- Avoid hairstyles that narrow the face

**Wide-set eyes:**
- Darker inner corner makeup
- Eyeliner extending inward

## The Limitations of Proportions

**Proportions are not everything:**
1. **Symmetry** often matters more than perfect proportions
2. **Skin quality** (clear, even tone) trumps mathematical ratios
3. **Facial harmony** (how features work together) is more important than individual measurements
4. **Expression and personality** affect attractiveness more than millimeters

**Use proportions as a tool, not a rule.**
