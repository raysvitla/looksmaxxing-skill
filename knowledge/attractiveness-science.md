# Attractiveness Science

## Core Principles

### 1. Averageness
- **Principle:** Faces close to population average are attractive
- **Why:** Signals genetic diversity, health, lack of mutations
- **But:** Some distinctiveness (in attractive directions) can enhance appeal

### 2. Symmetry
- **Principle:** Symmetrical faces are more attractive
- **Why:** Signals developmental stability, genetic quality
- **Effect size:** Moderate (important but not everything)

### 3. Sexual Dimorphism
- **Principle:** Sex-typical features enhance attractiveness
  - **Men:** Masculine features (strong jaw, prominent brow, facial hair)
  - **Women:** Feminine features (large eyes, full lips, small nose)
- **Why:** Signals hormones, fertility, genetic fitness

### 4. Neoteny (Youthful Features)
- **Principle:** Childlike features are attractive (especially in women)
  - Large eyes
  - Small nose
  - Full lips
  - Round face
  - Smooth skin
- **Why:** Signals youth, fertility, health

## Context-Dependent Factors

### Cultural Variation
- Preferences vary across cultures
- But core principles (symmetry, health) are universal

### Relationship Type
- **Short-term:** More masculine features (high testosterone)
- **Long-term:** More balanced features (trustworthiness, investment)

### Individual Variation
- Personal preferences vary widely
- What's attractive "on average" ≠ attractive to everyone

## The Halo Effect
- Attractive people are perceived as:
  - More intelligent
  - More successful
  - Kinder
  - More trustworthy
- This is a cognitive bias, not reality

## Pretty Privilege
- Attractive people receive:
  - Higher salaries
  - Better treatment in legal system
  - More social opportunities
  - Greater assumed competence
