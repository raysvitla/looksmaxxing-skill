# Face Shapes Guide

## The 6 Main Face Shapes

### 1. Oval
- Balanced proportions
- Forehead slightly wider than chin
- Gently rounded jawline
- **Best for:** Most hairstyles work
- **Avoid:** Styles that add too much volume (can elongate face)

### 2. Round
- Equal width and length
- Soft, curved jawline
- Full cheeks
- **Best for:** Angular styles, side parts, vertical volume
- **Avoid:** Blunt bobs at chin length, center parts (emphasize roundness)

### 3. Square
- Strong, angular jawline
- Forehead, cheekbones, and jaw similar width
- **Best for:** Soft, layered styles, side-swept bangs
- **Avoid:** Blunt, geometric cuts (too harsh)

### 4. Heart
- Wider forehead
- Narrow, pointed chin
- **Best for:** Chin-length styles, side-swept bangs
- **Avoid:** Heavy, full bangs (emphasize forehead width)

### 5. Oblong/Rectangle
- Length greater than width
- Straight sides
- **Best for:** Width-adding styles, side parts, waves
- **Avoid:** Long, straight styles (elongate further)

### 6. Diamond
- Narrow forehead and chin
- Wide cheekbones
- **Best for:** Side-swept styles, chin-length cuts
- **Avoid:** Volume at cheekbones

---

## Why This Framework is Limiting

**The "face shape" approach has problems:**
1. Most faces don't fit neatly into one category
2. It ignores individual features (hairline, jawline quality, symmetry)
3. It's overly simplistic

**Better approach:** Use the 6 features framework from `hairstyles-guide.md`

---

## When to Use Face Shapes

- Quick reference for beginners
- General guidelines when you don't have detailed feature analysis
- Starting point before refining with feature-based approach

**For precise recommendations:** Always use the 6 features method.
